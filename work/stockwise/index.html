01 / The problem

Why this
exists.

For a small retail business, the challenge is rarely a lack of data. It's that sales, stock, payments and people live in different places. A useful interface has to make every decision easier without hiding the details.

02 / The design response

Intentional
by design.

A documentary retail narrative leads into an interactive workspace. Industrial-citrus contrast and Archivo Black separate STOCKWISE from the existing ONE project; the interactive demo traces sample sales, payment records, stock delivery, customer balances and alerts without claiming real payment processing.

03 / Creative process

From idea
to interface.

01 / Structure

Map the important decisions

Give sales, inventory, invoices, and customers a single predictable navigation model.

02 / Visual system

Calm data, confident contrast

Use carbon-black type, electric-yellow action accents, documentary retail photography, and practical high-contrast data tables.

03 / Interaction

Make the numbers respond

A completed walk-in sale deducts stock immediately. Issuing an invoice leaves inventory unchanged until goods are handed over. Payment and fulfillment are recorded separately; movements leave an audit trail.

05 / Honest prototype notes

What is
really built.

The shop, customers and prices are fictional. Paid sales, invoice balances, stock movements and reorder alerts respond to the local demo. M-Pesa, cash and card are recorded labels only, not real payment integrations.

This is independent speculative portfolio work, not a commissioned client project. All eight device previews display responsive states of the actual HTML and JavaScript demo. The interactions can be tested in a full-screen browser. The case-study presentation pairs original coded brand artwork with illustrative Unsplash-licensed real photography. Photographed people do not represent or endorse these fictional businesses. No completed videos or live services are claimed.
